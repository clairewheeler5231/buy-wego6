A single daily capsule, by design

The whole of WEGO6 is one capsule a day. There is no loading week, no morning-and-evening split and no escalating amount, so the routine you start on day one is identical to the routine on day sixty.

That consistency is part of why it is easy to live with. Your body meets the same 387 mg at roughly the same time every day, which is about as predictable as a supplement routine can be.

It is also the reason the pack maths never gets complicated. Thirty capsules, thirty mornings, one carton per month: there is no scenario in which you find yourself with capsules left over and no idea how many you have actually taken, and no scenario in which the pack runs out early because the routine crept upwards.

The ingredients are ones people already know

The blend is built on plant-based ingredients, amino acids and zinc, with apple cider vinegar among the components named on the pack. There is nothing exotic on that list, and most of it is already familiar from kitchens and everyday nutrition.

Zinc is the recognised nutrient in the formula and contributes to normal macronutrient metabolism. The rest are plant-based ingredients and amino acids, which is precisely the sort of company you want in a supplement you plan to take every morning.

Taking WEGO6 comfortably

Take the capsule with a full glass of water and with food rather than on a completely empty stomach. Breakfast is the natural home for it, and the food does most of the work in keeping it comfortable.

The morning also makes better sense than the evening, simply because it is the half of the day you are most likely to be consistent in. If mornings are when you reliably eat something, that one small timing choice is worth making from the start.

Use a full glass rather than a mouthful. It is the detail most people skip and the one most worth keeping, because it helps the capsule go down cleanly and turns the whole business into a two-second job. That matters for something you intend to repeat thirty times a month.

Stick to the stated dose

One capsule a day is the dose, and there is no benefit in taking more. The formula is built around that single 387 mg serving, and a 30-capsule pack is designed to last exactly one month at that rate.

If you miss a day, simply carry on the next morning as normal. There is nothing to make up and nothing to double, which is one of the quieter advantages of a once-daily product.

There is equally little to be gained from saving capsules up for a better week. The pack is built around thirty consecutive mornings, and a carton stretched across six weeks of occasional use has simply produced a less complete picture than one that lasted the thirty days it was designed for.

One sensible note

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking any medication or living with a medical condition, have a quick word with your doctor before starting WEGO6, as you would with any supplement.

Otherwise, keep the pack somewhere dry and out of direct sunlight, keep it away from children, and take your capsule with breakfast. That is genuinely the whole of it.

The same applies if you are already taking something else daily and simply want to be sure everything sits together comfortably. A quick word with a pharmacist or your GP settles it, costs nothing, and means you can get on with the routine without wondering about it every morning.

What the first week usually looks like

The opening week with WEGO6 is mostly a matter of finding the capsule a permanent home in your morning. There is no build-up phase and no increasing amount, so day one and day seven are identical: one capsule, a full glass of water, taken with food.

Most people attach it to breakfast within three or four mornings and stop thinking about it after that. If your breakfast moves around, whether that is shift work, early trains or a household in which the mornings are chaos, attach it instead to something that never moves: the kettle going on, the first coffee, the moment you sit down at a desk.

Keeping the pack visible does most of the remaining work. A carton at the back of a cupboard is a decision to be made every single morning; a carton beside the mugs is not a decision at all, and that difference is what carries a routine through week three.

Timing and the shape of your day

The reason WEGO6 belongs to the morning rather than the evening is a practical one: mornings repeat more reliably than evenings do. Taking it early attaches the capsule to the most fixed part of your day, and that one choice, made on the first morning, settles the question for the whole run.

If your mornings vary a great deal, anchor the capsule to something that happens whatever time you get up: the kettle going on, the first thing you eat, the moment you sit down at a desk. Nothing more elaborate than that is needed, and plenty of people never think about it at all.

Beyond that, the day around the capsule does not need to change. Normal meals, normal movement, normal working hours: WEGO6 is designed to sit inside the routine you already have rather than to reorganise it around itself, and that is exactly why it is easy to keep going.

Weekends, travel and weeks that do not go to plan

A once-daily capsule is unusually robust against real life. A late start, a long journey or three nights away breaks nothing at all, because there is no schedule to fall behind and nothing to make up afterwards.

For travel the pack is the entire kit. A 30-capsule carton slips into a wash bag or a laptop sleeve, and the only thing to remember is to pack it next to something you will definitely open on the first morning, such as a toothbrush, a charger or a book.

If a day gets away from you completely, take the next capsule at your usual time the following morning and carry on through the pack as normal. One missed morning is not worth a second thought, and there is nothing to double up on to compensate for it.

Storing the pack sensibly

Keep the carton somewhere dry, at room temperature and out of direct sunlight. In most homes that means a kitchen cupboard rather than a windowsill, and well away from the steam of a kettle or the heat above a hob.

Keep the capsules in their own carton rather than decanting them into a tin or a pill organiser, because the pack carries the label, the batch details and the directions. Keep it out of reach of children, as you would with anything else on that shelf.

Handled that way there is nothing else to think about. The pack lasts the month it is meant to last, and the only date worth keeping an eye on is the one where it runs out and the next carton needs to be in the house.

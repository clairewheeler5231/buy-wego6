So the accurate answer is narrower than either “yes” or “scam”. The product is genuine. Some of the advertising circulating around it is not, and that is almost certainly why you are on this page.

Why you are probably asking this

Almost nobody searches whether a supplement is a scam out of idle curiosity. People search it because they saw something that felt wrong — usually an advert claiming a television appearance, a celebrity backer, or a doctor’s endorsement.

Those adverts are not made by the manufacturer, and the claims in them are not true. There has been no television appearance and there is no celebrity endorsement. If that is what brought you here, your instinct was correct: the advert was misleading. What it does not tell you is whether the product behind it exists, and that is a separate question with a separate answer.

It is worth being blunt about the shape of this market. Weight-management supplements attract this kind of advertising because the audience is large and motivated, and because an unaffiliated party can run an advert about any brand without the brand’s involvement. Seeing a fake advert for a product tells you about the person who made the advert. It does not, by itself, tell you about the product.

Four things you can check yourself, before spending anything

Every one of these is a matter of record rather than an opinion, and all four can be confirmed in about a minute without paying for anything.

What is in it. The pack declares 387 mg of active blend per capsule and lists its components. Apple cider vinegar and zinc are named by the manufacturer; the full list is printed on the carton. What happens if you change your mind. A 60-day money-back window, with a stated procedure rather than a slogan. That is covered in full further down this page.

What WEGO 6 is not

Being clear about this now matters more than it looks. Most of the anger aimed at supplements comes from a gap between what an advert implied and what the product was ever going to do. Closing that gap before you buy is the difference between a purchase you are content with and one you resent.
